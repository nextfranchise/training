The Multiplier Effect

Why Consulting Mistakes Cost More in Franchising

A mistake in a regular business hurts that single business. A mistake in a franchise system is copied into every branch sold.

Franchisees are frequently OFWs returning home, retirees, or families putting up their life savings. If a consultant copies an unchecked ticket price or an unproven break-even, the franchisor doesn't just fail — dozens of families lose everything.

Case Study in Action

Dino Crew PH: The Training Standoff

A real Next Franchise engagement from November 2026. How would you solve it?

The Situation (Part 1)

Dino Crew PH runs dinosaur mascot shows, party entertainment, and claw/gachapon rentals. Its Franchise Agreement mandates that every new hire must complete online LMS modules and be certified before working any event. The platform was set up, and monthly royalties cover it. In November 2026, franchisee training had not started.

The Traditional Consultant Move

Send a Legal Memo & Penalty Schedule

Issue a formal warning quoting training and audit clauses from the contract. The rule is legally correct, but nothing changes because no one asked why training had not started.

The Next Franchise Answer

Ground Commando & Root Blocker

Meet franchisees on the ground first. Ask how they train teams today and what gets in the way. Treat "they are not interested" as an unverified assumption.

The contract clause is kept as the floor, rather than the first move.

Meta Skills at Work: Customer Obsession & Ground Commando (start from franchisee's day), First Principles (every reason is an assumption), Power Law (fix the one blocker), Relentless Execution (one next proof, champion, date).

Key Takeaways for Module 1

  • 1. The Franchise Multiplier: In franchising, an uncorrected error is copied into every sold unit at the expense of family savings.
  • 2. Habits Over Documents: The traditional answer produces a memo that is ignored. The NF answer visits the ground and alters what happens at the next event.
  • 3. Contracts are Floors: Legal clauses protect the brand as a last resort, never as the primary tool of problem solving.
